# How To Play Dota Auto Chess

A from-scratch primer, written by cross-referencing this project's own game
data against a handful of guide videos (credited below) rather than any
built-in knowledge of the game — so treat this as a living document to
correct as the meta shifts.

## The core loop

Eight players share a lobby. Every round you either fight a wave of PvE
creeps (rounds 1–3, then every 5th round after) or get matched against
another player's board. You buy units from a shared 5-slot shop, place them
on your board, and combat plays out automatically based on stats, synergies,
and items — there's no unit control mid-fight, all the decisions happen in
the shop and on positioning between rounds.

Units come in cost tiers (1 through 5). Buying three copies of the same unit
fuses them into a 2-star version (roughly double stats), and three 2-stars
fuse into a 3-star (routinely the single biggest power spike available to
you). Each unit also carries one or two synergy tags — a species/origin tag
and a class tag — and stacking copies of *different* units that share a tag
unlocks board-wide bonuses at set thresholds (e.g. Naga at 2/4/6, Monk at
2/4/6, Assassin at 3/6/9).

## Economy

Gold does three things: buys units, buys XP to level up (raising your unit
cap and the odds of higher-tier units showing up in the shop), and — if left
unspent — generates interest. Every 10 gold banked at the end of a round
pays 1 interest, capping at 5 bonus gold/round once you're sitting on 50+.
Winning or losing a combat round also swings your gold: consecutive wins or
losses build a streak bonus, and streaking (either direction) beats yo-yoing
between the two, since bouncing back and forth forfeits the streak bonus
each time.

Practical guidance from the source videos: hold at 50 gold when you can
afford to, but stop banking hard once you're deep in the mid-game (~round
21+), when the field turns aggressive and idle gold stops being worth more
than board strength.

## Rerolling, leveling, and "banning" (removing units from the pool)

The shop's odds of offering higher-cost units improve as you level. Lower
levels (3–5) roll mostly tier-1/tier-2 units at good rates — this is the
window for "hyper-rolling" a cheap, aggressive comp before other players
catch up. Every unit type has a limited shared pool (roughly 45 copies of a
1-cost, fewer as cost rises), so contested picks matter.

You can spend gold to remove a unit or unit-tag entirely from the shared
pool for a while (referred to as "banning" or "bending" a unit/synergy in
the source videos) — useful once you know exactly what you're hunting for
and want to stop wasting rolls on unrelated tiers, or want to bias the pool
toward the copies you still need.

## Win-streak vs. lose-streak

Deciding which lane you're in early (ideally by round 5) matters more than
which specific build you land on:

- **Win-streak**: stack early tankiness so you don't drop the streak by
  accident. Vanguard → Tranquil Boots → Buckler on one front-line melee unit
  is the commonly cited cheap, unkillable-early-game combo. Relics that
  convert HP to gold (you'll have HP to spare) are a good pickup here.
- **Lose-streak**: keep 2 Priests on the board even while intentionally
  fielding a weak lineup — Priests still generate healing/tangoes while you
  eat losses on purpose. Relics that convert HP loss into bonus XP
  (Malefic Rapture) are the centerpiece of a lose-streak plan, since they
  let you hit level 8–9 far ahead of schedule.

## Relics — a working reference

Relics are offered periodically (first at round 10) and, once picked, stay
active for a fixed number of rounds *from the round you actually select
them* — so it's often correct to leave the relic choice unresolved for
several rounds while you wait for the moment you're about to lose a streak
(and want the streak-protection relic to start its clock as late as
possible), then lock it in right before that round. Caveat: while a relic
choice sits unresolved, don't open neutral-monster item boxes in the same
window — the two queued choices interact badly and can cost you a pick.

Recurring picks worth knowing early:

- **Weighted Dice / Lucid Star** — accelerate 3-starring (see the
  [Naga/Monks build](/builds) for how these get used in practice).
- **Second Chance** — rerolls into tier-3 items specifically, good when
  you're missing one key item rather than gold in general.
- **Chiaroscuro Certainty** ("the certainty loop") — protects your current
  win/lose streak for 10 rounds from the moment you pick it; timing the
  pick for the round right before you'd otherwise break streak is the whole
  trick.
- **Stolen Vault** — no build-defining upside, but a safe ~10–30 gold
  floor when nothing else on offer is worth taking.
- **Malefic Rapture** — converts HP loss into bonus XP; the backbone of
  most deliberate lose-streak plans.
- **Magic Seed** — spawns bench "seeds" that individually grow in gold
  value over time; strong if your build doesn't need the bench space to
  hyper-roll after round 15.
- **Mango Tree** — rewards *alternating* win/loss results (not streaking)
  with stacking bonus gold/XP, capped off by a hidden guaranteed-survival
  effect once you've completed enough alternations. Contradicts the
  win-streak/lose-streak advice above by design — it's its own third
  approach.
- **Doubloon** — converts whichever of HP/mana/gold is currently highest
  into gold; a strong early hyper-boost specifically when HP is high and
  safe to spend, but easy to misuse by pressing it twice and converting
  gold right back into a worse resource.
- **Gaoler's Master Key** — lets you ban/un-ban synergies freely and
  refunds prior ban costs; good at any stage if the ban you want costs more
  than Stolen Vault would've given you.
- **March of the Beasts** ("double neutral monsters") — high risk: skip
  unless your board can already comfortably clear doubled PvE waves.
- **Wheel of Wonder** — grants one unit a bonus synergy tag of your choice;
  best saved for a specific late-game combo rather than used on reflex.

## Ranking strategy

The ladder runs Knight → Bishop → Rook → Queen → King → Queen (Queen is the
top rank). Matchmaking quality matters: once you've outgrown a rank
bracket, searching that bracket's lobby can leave you facing much lower-
ranked opponents, which skews the rating math against you (bigger losses,
smaller wins). Private-lobby passcodes for the higher brackets are one way
to find a properly-ranked population when public queues thin out.

Beyond mechanics, the recurring advice across these videos is to keep 3–4
proven builds in rotation rather than one, and to actively scout opponents
(hotkey `F3` in-client) before committing — both what they're holding and
what's sitting on their bench — so you can pivot away from whatever's
heavily contested and into something the lobby isn't already fighting over.
